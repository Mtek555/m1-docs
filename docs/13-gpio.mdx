## Electrical safety first {#electrical-safety}

:::danger[3.3 V logic]
Keep external signals at or below **3.3 V** unless the final production-board specification explicitly confirms a higher tolerance for the exact pin and mode. Incorrect voltage, current, polarity, or wiring can damage both devices.
:::

- When the GPIO menu opens, controllable pins start as outputs at **Low**.
- Leaving the GPIO menu returns the pins to a safe state and switches off external rails.
- The 3.3 V and 5 V rails cannot be enabled at the same time.
- The **USB-UART bridge** entry is present but not functional in the documented firmware.

## GPIO menu {#gpio-menu}

| Menu item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| GPIO Control | Selects a documented pin and toggles its output Low or High. |
| 3.3V power | Enables the 3.3 V rail and disables the 5 V rail. |
| 5V power | Enables the 5 V rail and disables the 3.3 V rail. |
| USB-UART bridge | Placeholder for a future feature; not currently functional. |

## Control a pin {#control-pin}

1. Open **GPIO > GPIO Control**.
2. Use **Left / Right** to select a controllable signal.
3. Press **Center** to toggle the output between Low and High.
4. Press **Back** when finished so the M1 can return pins and rails to their exit state.

The documented control screen includes `PE2`, `PE4`, `PE5`, `PE6`, `PD12`, `PD13`, `PA14`, `PA13`, `PC2`, `PC3`, `PD0`, and `PD1`. `PA9` and `PA10` are reserved for the planned serial bridge.

## Power-rail limits {#power-rails}

- Use the rails only for small, low-current peripherals.
- Do not power motors, solenoids, relays, heaters, or other high-current loads directly from the M1.
- Do not feed external power back into an enabled M1 rail.
- Use a common ground between the M1 and the external circuit.
- Treat `PC2` and `PC3` as 3.3 V maximum when used as analog inputs.

:::warning[Current limit not yet published]
The available documentation does not establish complete production-header or external-rail current limits. Do not publish or rely on a numeric limit until engineering confirms it.
:::
