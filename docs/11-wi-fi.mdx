## Before you start {#before}

<GuideHtml html={"<div class=\"callout warning\"><strong>Authorized use</strong><p>For legal and authorized use only. Use Wi-Fi tools only on networks you own or are authorized to test.</p></div><p>Wi-Fi includes tools for viewing nearby networks, checking channel activity, capturing diagnostic data, and running focused network tests.</p><p>An access point (AP) provides the Wi-Fi network. Its SSID is the network name, its BSSID is the access point’s unique address, and RSSI indicates received signal strength. Less-negative RSSI values indicate a stronger signal.</p><p>Exit Bluetooth before opening Wi-Fi. Insert a writable card for saved networks, handshakes, beacon lists, or PCAPNG packet-capture files.</p>"} />

## Networks {#networks}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>Networks scans nearby 2.4 GHz access points and lets you review, save, and inspect their associated devices.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → Networks</div><ol><li>Wait for the AP scan or cached list.</li><li>Use Left and Right to move through the network results; use Rescan for a fresh result.</li><li>Press Center on Actions.</li><li>Choose Scan for Devices to look for associated stations.</li><li>Choose Save Network to add or update SSID, BSSID, channel, RSSI, and security.</li><li>Choose Network Details for the full AP summary.</li><li>Choose Saved Networks to inspect the SD list.</li><li>In Saved Networks, choose Actions → Delete and confirm the intended entry.</li></ol><div class=\"callout note\"><strong>Saved network</strong><p>The saved entry includes the network name, address, channel, signal strength, and security type. It does not include the password.</p></div>"} />

## Deauth Client {#deauth}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>Deauth Client performs a short disconnect test against a selected device.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → Deauth Client</div><ol><li>Choose Use Selected when the correct AP is already selected, or Scan Networks.</li><li>Open the selected AP’s Actions.</li><li>Choose Target Devices, or Details to verify the AP first.</li><li>Select the exact station and review Device Details.</li><li>Verify network and device on the confirmation screen.</li><li>Choose Start and keep the test brief.</li><li>Press Center or Back to stop.</li><li>Use Change Device, Change Network, or Rescan before another test.</li></ol><div class=\"callout note\"><strong>Short connection test</strong><p>Deauth Client briefly disconnects the selected device from its network. Use it for authorized testing and stop when you have the result you need.</p></div>"} />

## MonstaShark {#monstashark}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>MonstaShark records Wi-Fi traffic heard on a single 2.4 GHz channel and saves it as a PCAPNG packet-capture file. The file can be opened directly in Wireshark.</p><p>Selecting a network sets the capture channel. The resulting file can contain traffic from any nearby network or device using that channel—it is not limited to the selected network. Protected traffic remains encrypted.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → MonstaShark</div><h3>Starting a capture</h3><ol><li>Insert a writable microSD card.</li><li>Open <b>MonstaShark</b> and wait for the network list.</li><li>Use Left and Right to browse networks. Press Down to run a fresh scan if needed.</li><li>Press Center to select a network.</li><li>Check the network name and channel.</li><li>Use Up or Down to choose 10, 30, or 60 seconds.</li><li>Press Center on <b>Run</b>.</li></ol><h3>During the capture</h3><p>The screen shows the channel, elapsed and total time, capture progress, frames recorded, and data written in KB.</p><p>The capture ends when the selected time expires. Press Center to stop early. Back does not stop an active MonstaShark capture.</p><h3>Reviewing the result</h3><p>Use Left or Right to switch between the two result pages.</p><div class=\"table-wrap\"><table><thead><tr><th>Page</th><th>What it shows</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>Channel, SD status, frames saved, file size, and file path.</td></tr><tr><td>2</td><td>Capture diagnostics, including dropped frames, transport errors, data loss, and whether the capture stopped cleanly.</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>Press Back to return to the network list.</p><h3>Capture files</h3><p>MonstaShark saves each capture automatically as <code>wifi/mcap_NNN.pcapng</code>. The number increases for each file, and existing captures are not overwritten.</p><ol><li>Exit MonstaShark and open <b>USB → USB Drive</b>.</li><li>Connect the M1 to the computer and open the <code>wifi</code> folder.</li><li>Copy the <code>.pcapng</code> file.</li><li>Eject the M1 before leaving USB Drive.</li><li>Open the copied file in Wireshark.</li></ol><div class=\"callout note\"><strong>Manual channel selection</strong><p>If no networks are found, manual mode starts on channel 6. Left and Right change channels 1–14, Up and Down select 10, 30, or 60 seconds, Center starts the capture, and Back exits. Confirm the correct channel before starting.</p></div><h3>MonstaShark troubleshooting</h3><div class=\"table-wrap\"><table><thead><tr><th>Screen or result</th><th>What to do</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Radio busy (BLE)</td><td>Leave Bluetooth completely, then retry.</td></tr><tr><td>No reply from ESP</td><td>Leave Wi-Fi, reopen it, and try again.</td></tr><tr><td>SD ERR</td><td>Confirm the card is mounted, has free space, and is not being used by USB Drive.</td></tr><tr><td>No frames recorded</td><td>Confirm the channel and retry closer to the network while traffic is active.</td></tr><tr><td>Drops or loss reported</td><td>Try a shorter capture and reduce nearby radio activity if possible.</td></tr><tr><td><code>no-stop</code> shown</td><td>The capture did not finish its normal closing sequence. Start a fresh capture before relying on the file.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>"} />

## Handshake {#handshake}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>A WPA handshake is the four-message exchange used when a device joins a protected Wi-Fi network. Handshake listens for that exchange and labels the four messages M1, M2, M3, and M4.</p><p>While the capture is running, the M1 sends short reconnect requests to the selected network. Connected devices may briefly disconnect and reconnect. No individual client needs to be selected.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → Handshake</div><h3>Selecting the network</h3><ol><li>Open <b>Handshake</b> and wait for the network list.</li><li>Use Left and Right to browse networks. Press Down to run a fresh scan if needed.</li><li>Press Center to select the network.</li><li>Check the displayed network name and channel.</li><li>Choose <b>Start Capture</b>. Choose <b>Change Network</b> if the wrong network is displayed.</li></ol><h3>Watching the capture</h3><p>The first screen shows the selected network and channel while the M1 waits for authentication activity.</p><p>When capture data becomes available, the screen shows M1, M2, M3, and M4. A filled stage means that message was detected. A complete capture contains all four messages. The packet meter shows stored capture data; it is not a completion percentage.</p><p>Press Center or Back to stop. A capture stopped manually is not saved; the next screen offers Retry or Back.</p><h3>Complete capture</h3><p>When all four messages are detected, the M1 displays <b>Handshake Captured</b>. Press Center to save. Pressing Back opens a choice between <b>Save Capture</b> and <b>Discard</b>.</p><h3>Incomplete capture</h3><p>If some authentication data was collected but all four messages were not detected, the M1 displays <b>Capture Incomplete</b>.</p><ul><li><b>Save Capture</b> keeps the partial result.</li><li><b>Retry</b> starts another attempt with the same network.</li><li><b>Back</b> returns without saving.</li></ul><p>If no data was collected, the M1 displays <b>No handshake data</b> and offers Retry or Back.</p><h3>Saved files</h3><p>The M1 asks for a filename before saving the capture as <code>wifi/handshake_&lt;name&gt;.txt</code>. Existing files are not overwritten.</p><p>Handshake files are text records exported by the firmware. They are not PCAPNG files and do not open in Wireshark like MonstaShark captures. Use MonstaShark when you need a general packet capture. Capturing a handshake does not reveal or verify the network password.</p><h3>Handshake troubleshooting</h3><div class=\"table-wrap\"><table><thead><tr><th>Screen or result</th><th>What to do</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>No networks found</td><td>Move closer and press Down to rescan.</td></tr><tr><td>Network unavailable</td><td>Rescan and select the network again.</td></tr><tr><td>Select failed</td><td>Rescan; the network list may no longer match the current environment.</td></tr><tr><td>Waiting for handshake</td><td>Keep the M1 near the network and allow an authorized device to reconnect.</td></tr><tr><td>Capture incomplete</td><td>Retry, or save the partial result if it is useful for the test.</td></tr><tr><td>No handshake data</td><td>Confirm the network and channel, then retry while a device reconnects.</td></tr><tr><td>Save failed</td><td>Check that the microSD card is mounted, writable, and has free space.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>"} />

## Beacon {#beacon}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>Beacon transmits test network names over 2.4 GHz from an SD list or a name entered on the keypad.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → Beacon</div><div class=\"table-wrap\"><table><thead><tr><th>Source</th><th>Instructions</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>SD List</td><td>Put one SSID per line in wifi/ap_lists.txt; up to 16 non-empty entries, 32 bytes each.</td></tr><tr><td>Keypad SSID</td><td>Enter one SSID and verify it before transmission.</td></tr></tbody></table></div><ol><li>Select the source.</li><li>Review the running names and count.</li><li>Use it in a controlled test environment and stop when finished.</li><li>Press Center or Back to stop.</li><li>Wait for Stopping before leaving.</li></ol><div class=\"callout note\"><strong>Fallback</strong><p>If the SD list is empty, the M1 may use the currently selected network name. Check the running screen.</p></div>"} />

## Channel Survey {#survey}

<GuideHtml html={"<p>Channel Survey summarizes nearby 2.4 GHz activity by channel using access-point count and strongest signal.</p><div class=\"menu-path\"><strong>Menu path</strong>Main Menu → Wi-Fi → Channel Survey</div><ol><li>Wait for the one-shot 2.4 GHz scan.</li><li>Read total detected APs.</li><li>Use Left and Right to move through channels 1–14.</li><li>Compare AP count and strongest RSSI.</li><li>Press Center to rescan.</li><li>Press Back to exit.</li></ol><p>Fewer APs does not automatically mean a better channel; consider signal strength, width, interference, and regulatory domain.</p>"} />

## Troubleshooting {#troubleshooting}

<GuideHtml html={"<div class=\"table-wrap\"><table><thead><tr><th>Problem</th><th>What to do</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>ESP32 not ready</td><td>Leave, try bridge reset, and reopen Wi-Fi.</td></tr><tr><td>Radio busy</td><td>Leave Bluetooth completely.</td></tr><tr><td>No APs</td><td>Move closer, rescan, and confirm supported 2.4 GHz channels.</td></tr><tr><td>No devices</td><td>Rescan; the station may be idle, randomized, or unassociated.</td></tr><tr><td>Capture link failed</td><td>Retry once after leaving BLE; then verify ESP32 firmware pairing.</td></tr><tr><td>Save failed</td><td>Check mount, free space, and USB Drive ownership.</td></tr><tr><td>Handshake incomplete</td><td>Verify channel and activity; stay inside the approved window.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>"} />
